Methodology — V3 Well-Anchored Geostatistical

Random Forest + Residual Kriging • Iowa Shallow Geothermal Suitability

Architectural Shift from V2

V2 used a flat weighted sum of four independently scored layers, treating every pixel as an isolated assessment. V3 replaces this with a geostatistical interpolation model where well observations are the ground truth and geological layers guide spatial extrapolation.

Core principle: Wells are the only dataset that integrates depth, lithology, saturation, and yield at a single point. Every other layer provides partial, indirect evidence. V3 asks: given what nearby wells in similar geological settings tell us, what is the predicted aquifer suitability at this unsampled location?

Well Productivity Scoring (Response Variable)

Each of ~7,300 Iowa DNR Sourcewater wells receives a composite productivity score (0–1) computed as a weighted sum of eight field-level scores using percentile normalization within the shallow well population:

FieldWeightScoring
Pump Test GPM25%Percentile: p90+=1.0, p75=0.8, p50=0.6, p25=0.3, <p10=0.1
Specific Capacity20%Percentile: same breakpoints
Transmissivity15%Log-scale percentile normalization
Aquifer Type15%Alluvial=1.0, Sand&Gravel=0.9, Drift=0.7, Bedrock=0.2
Aquifer Thickness10%≥50ft=1.0, 30-50=0.8, 15-30=0.5, <15=0.2
Well Depth5%50-150ft=1.0, 20-50=0.6, 150-250=0.4
Static Water Level5%<20ft=1.0, 20-50=0.8, 50-100=0.5, >100=0.2
Confined/Unconfined5%Unconfined=1.0, Confined=0.6

Wells with null fields receive neutral scores (0.4–0.5) for those fields. A data completeness index (0–5) tracks how many productivity fields are populated.

Random Forest Trend Model

A RandomForestRegressor (200 trees, max_depth=12) predicts well productivity scores from five geospatial covariates:

CovariateSourceRole
Surficial Geology ScoreIowa DNR 100k MapServerPrimary interpolation zone layer
Depth-to-Bedrock ScoreIGS contour interpolationDrilling window constraint
Landform Region ScoreIowa DNR + well-derived averagesRegional geological context
Alluvial Aquifer MembershipUSGS Principal AquifersBinary aquifer presence
Distance to Alluvial BoundaryDerived from aquifer polygonsProximity to productive aquifers

Wells with higher data completeness receive higher sample weights during training. The model is evaluated with 5-fold cross-validation.

Residual Kriging

After RF prediction, residuals (observed score minus RF prediction) are computed at each well location. These residuals capture local variation not explained by the covariates — e.g., a well that is more productive than its geological setting would suggest.

An empirical variogram (spherical model) is fitted to the residuals, and ordinary kriging interpolates them onto a ~2 km statewide grid. The kriging also produces a variance surface that quantifies prediction uncertainty.

Final Suitability Surface

Suitability(x) = RF_prediction(x) + Kriged_residual(x)
    clipped to [0, 1], scaled to 0–100

Confidence(x) = 1 / (1 + Kriging_variance(x))
    High near wells, low in data-sparse areas

Constraint mask: EPA Superfund sites (500m buffer) = 0.

Key Advantages Over V2

  • Wells as ground truth, not just another layer. V2 treated well data as one of four inputs. V3 treats wells as the response variable that all other layers exist to support.
  • Actual aquifer properties in scoring. V3 uses transmissivity, specific capacity, aquifer thickness, and hydraulic conductivity with percentile normalization.
  • Geological layers inform interpolation, not scoring. V2 assigned arbitrary scores to landform regions. V3 learns the actual relationship between geological setting and productivity from well data.
  • Built-in uncertainty. V2 produced a single score. V3 produces both a prediction and a confidence surface from kriging variance.

Site Inquiry (Click Report)

Clicking any location shows the predicted suitability score, prediction confidence (from kriging variance), covariate values, and nearest wells with their productivity data. The confidence level indicates how well the model knows that location — high near dense well clusters, low in data-sparse uplands.

Limitations

  • Model trained on ~7,300 public water supply wells. Private wells, monitoring wells, and irrigation wells are excluded.
  • Surficial geology uses 1:100,000 scale mapping (~20% coverage at higher resolution). V3 uses statewide coverage with reduced detail.
  • Kriging assumes spatial stationarity. Geological boundaries may violate this assumption.
  • This is a regional screening tool. Site-specific geotechnical investigation is required before installation.
