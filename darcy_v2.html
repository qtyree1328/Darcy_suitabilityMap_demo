Methodology — V2 Darcy-Aligned

Iowa Darcy-Style Shallow Geothermal Suitability Screening

Overview

This map presents a Darcy-aligned multi-criteria geothermal suitability assessment for Iowa, designed specifically for shallow geothermal screening. The model targets surficial aquifers shallower than ~150 ft, consistent with Darcy Solutions' closed-loop U-line system that uses heat exchangers submerged in shallow groundwater. The final score (0–100) is a weighted linear combination of four normalized factor scores, dominated by ground-truth aquifer productivity data.

Key Design Principle

Ground-truth well data drives 65% of the suitability score. Rather than using spatial buffers (which create circular blobs and gaps), well productivity is propagated through geologic similarity: wells in each Iowa landform region are averaged, and that average applies to the entire geologic province. Where local wells exist nearby, they refine the regional score. The result is a continuous, gap-free map anchored by measured hydrogeologic data.

Well Properties & Scoring

Each of the 7,299 Iowa DNR Sourcewater public wells is scored 0–1 using a weighted average of up to 8 hydrogeologic parameters extracted from the well record:

Final well score = weighted average of all available signals (missing fields are skipped, not penalized). High pump test GPM, high transmissivity, shallow unconsolidated aquifers score highest — directly measuring "is this a productive shallow aquifer for heat exchange?"

Geologic Propagation

Well scores are propagated to surrounding areas through geologic similarity, not spatial buffers or kernel density:

  • Each of the 7,299 DNR Sourcewater wells is spatially assigned to its Iowa DNR Landform Region — a geologic province defined by shared depositional history, surficial material, and hydrogeomorphic character (10 provinces, 28 polygons covering all of Iowa).
  • The regional productivity for each province is the average of all well productivity scores within it. Examples:
    • Missouri River Alluvial Plain: 191 wells → avg 0.873
    • Des Moines Lobe: 1,176 wells → avg 0.747
    • Southern Iowa Drift Plain: 2,646 wells → avg 0.751
    • Paleozoic Plateau: 609 wells → avg 0.614
  • This average is painted onto the entire geologic province — no gaps, no circular buffers, no fallback values. Areas without nearby wells receive the measured average of wells in the same geology.
  • This works because wells within the same geologic province share the same surficial geology, depositional history, and aquifer characteristics. A well's productivity is representative of its geologic context.

Suitability Equation

Score = 0.650 × AquiferProductivity
       + 0.150 × DepthToBedrock
       + 0.100 × LandCover
       + 0.100 × Constraints

AquiferProductivity = avg(well_productivity_score) per geologic province
    (painted from Iowa DNR Landform Regions, no spatial buffers)

Output: 0–100 (each factor normalized 0–1, then scaled)

Color Ramp

The map overlay uses histogram equalization (p2–p98) to stretch the actual data distribution across the full color palette. This maximizes visual contrast without changing the underlying data.

Site Inquiry (Click Report)

Clicking any location in Iowa generates a site inquiry report. The overall score is a weighted sum of four factor scores computed client-side from loaded vector data (aquifer productivity from the geologic province's well-derived average, depth-to-bedrock zone, EPA buffers). Nearby wells are shown as context but the score comes from the geologic unit, not spatial proximity. These are approximate and intended for rapid screening, not engineering decisions.

Limitations

  • Regional productivity averages are derived from 7,299 public water supply wells, not private wells. Regions with <3 wells fall back to hand-estimated landform hydro scores.
  • Geologic propagation assumes wells within the same landform region share similar aquifer characteristics. This is a regional approximation — local variation within a province is smoothed.
  • Depth-to-bedrock is interpolated from 50-ft contour intervals; precision is limited between contours.
  • This is a regional screening tool. Site-specific geotechnical investigation is required before installation.
